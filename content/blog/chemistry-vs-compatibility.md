Two things get measured in a first conversation, and people routinely mistake one for the other.

**Chemistry** is the immediate sense that this is enjoyable — that talking is easy, that attention is mutual, that you would like to stay. It is fast, physical, and arrives before you know anything factual about the person.

**Compatibility** is whether two lives can actually be built alongside each other. Pace, values, money, family, location, how conflict is handled, what a normal week looks like. It is slow, unglamorous, and mostly invisible at the start.

Neither is a substitute for the other, and the usual failure is treating whichever one showed up first as the whole answer.

## What chemistry is good for

Chemistry is not trivial and it is not a distraction. Dismissing attraction as shallow is its own mistake — it is what makes the effort of getting to know someone feel worth making, and a relationship with none of it is a friendship with extra logistics.

It tells you reliably: that you enjoy this person's company, that attention flows both ways, that you are curious.

It tells you nothing about: whether they are kind under pressure, whether they follow through, whether your lives fit, or whether they want what you want.

Chemistry also has a specific failure mode: it is heightened by uncertainty. Intensity that comes from not knowing where you stand is easy to mistake for depth. If the feeling drops sharply the moment things become secure, that is worth noticing.

## What compatibility is good for

Compatibility is what determines whether an enjoyable connection becomes a workable relationship. It is also much harder to read early, because most of it concerns behaviour you have not yet observed.

It tells you: whether the practical shape of your lives can coexist, whether your expectations of a relationship resemble each other, whether disagreement is survivable.

It tells you nothing about: whether you will enjoy each other. Two highly compatible people can be perfectly matched on paper and flat in person. That is a real outcome, and no amount of alignment fixes it.

## Where they overlap

The distinction is useful but not clean.

Some things read as chemistry and are actually early compatibility: someone asking a follow-up question and remembering the answer; finding the same things funny, which is often shared values arriving in disguise; being at ease with silence.

And compatibility can create chemistry over time. People become more attractive as they become more known. This is one reason a lukewarm first meeting is worth a second — a point that cuts against how most people use first impressions.

## What a first conversation can and cannot establish

**It can establish:** whether talking is easy, whether they ask as well as answer, what they are visibly enthusiastic about, how they treat people around them, and whether they can be disagreed with pleasantly.

**It cannot establish:** whether they are reliable, how they behave under stress, whether they want children, whether your finances or families are workable, or whether you would be happy in two years.

This matters practically. A first meeting is a screen, not a verdict. Used as a screen it works well. Used as a verdict it produces two errors at once: over-investing in strong chemistry and discarding people who were merely nervous.

Nerves, in particular, are the most common reason a good conversation does not happen — and they say nothing about compatibility.

## Questions that are actually useful

These are sample questions, with what a response can and cannot tell you. **They are not a test and there are no correct answers** — the value is in what gets revealed incidentally.

**"What does an ordinary week look like for you?"**
*Tells you:* how they actually spend time, how much of it is committed, whether their life has room in it.
*Cannot tell you:* whether they would make room. People reorganise for things they want.

**"What's something you've changed your mind about?"**
*Tells you:* whether they can hold a position loosely, and whether they find being wrong tolerable.
*Cannot tell you:* whether they will be reasonable in an argument that concerns them personally.

**"What are you looking forward to this year?"**
*Tells you:* their actual direction, in their own words, without the loaded framing of "where do you see yourself in five years".
*Cannot tell you:* whether their plans and yours can share a city.

**"What do people usually get wrong about you?"**
*Tells you:* self-awareness, and how they handle a question with no prepared answer.
*Cannot tell you:* whether their self-assessment is accurate. It is one of the least reliable things anyone reports.

**"What's the last thing you disagreed with someone about?"**
*Tells you:* a great deal about how they narrate conflict — whether the other person is a character or a villain.
*Cannot tell you:* who was right, and it is not an invitation to adjudicate.

**"How do you usually spend time with your family?"**
*Tells you:* proximity, obligation, warmth, and how much of life is already committed.
*Cannot tell you:* what their family will make of you.

A note on delivery: asked in a row these become an interview. Asked once, in passing, and followed up on, they become a conversation.

## Reading the pair together

A rough guide, held loosely:

- **Chemistry high, compatibility unknown.** The normal, promising state after a first meeting. Meet again. Find out.
- **Chemistry high, compatibility clearly poor** — different countries, different intentions, incompatible plans. This is where people lose years. The feeling is real; it does not resolve the facts.
- **Chemistry low, compatibility high.** Worth a second meeting, particularly if either of you was nervous. Worth not forcing past two or three.
- **Both low.** Straightforward. Little is gained by persisting on principle.

What cannot be done is predicting longevity. There is no reliable way to determine from early conversation whether a relationship will last. Anyone offering a compatibility percentage is offering a number, not a finding.

## The practical upshot

Use chemistry to decide whether to spend more time. Use compatibility to decide whether to build something. Do not let either overrule the other too early, and give the slow signal enough time to arrive — most of it cannot appear in one conversation.
